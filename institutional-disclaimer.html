Status & reliance

Independent means this site can report, but it cannot decide.

This is a student-maintained resource. It is not a publication, service, or representation of Northeastern University or Northeastern University School of Law, and nothing here is an official statement of what the university requires of you. This page sets out what that distinction means in practice — what the site checks, what it will not resolve, and which office answers which question.

What the difference actually changes

The practical difference is not a legal formality. It is this: an official page can create or change an obligation. This site can only observe one.

When an official Northeastern page says a deadline is 15 February, that deadline exists because the university set it. When this site says the same thing, it is reporting an observation made on a particular day, from a particular page, by one student. If the university changes the page tomorrow, the obligation changes tomorrow — and this site is wrong until someone notices.

That is why every material claim here should carry a source link and a date, and why the instruction to confirm before acting is not boilerplate. It is the honest description of what a peer resource can offer.

A conflict this site will not resolve for you

The clearest illustration is a live one. Two official Northeastern pages currently describe the FlexJD relevant-work-experience requirement differently:

  • The FlexJD academics page lists one semester-long co-op with a concurrent reflections seminar totalling 8 credits, one additional semester-long practical experience of at least 275 hours during a non-academic semester, and “an additional 1,000 hours of relevant work experience.”
  • The JD degree requirements page lists the part-time (FlexJD) co-op requirement as co-ops corresponding to two terms, a field placement and associated seminar, and “350 Hours of Relevant Work Experience.”

Both pages were open and read on 29 August 2026 and both said what is quoted above. This site is not going to pick the more convenient number, average them, or quietly print one and drop the other. It records the conflict and points you at the office that can state the controlling requirement for your class year: Academic and Student Affairs.

Treat that as the general rule. Where official sources disagree, the conflict is preserved, not smoothed.

Reliance limits, by kind of information

Program requirements and academic policy
Reported from official pages with a review date. Requirements differ by class year and can change mid-programme. Confirm anything that affects graduation, registration, or credit with Academic and Student Affairs before you act on it.
Deadlines and dates
Relative wording such as “next week” is replaced with a date. A date on this site is the date the source published, on the day it was read. Published dates on official pages are frequently marked subject to change; confirm before you plan travel, time off, or payment around one.
Opportunity listings
Leads, not endorsements and not guarantees. The sponsor, employer, court, agency, or fellowship controls eligibility, deadline, compensation, location, and process, and can change any of them without notice. Apply from the sponsor's own page.
Contacts and phone numbers
Taken from official directories and reviewed each academic term. People move. If a number does not reach the office named, the university's main line will route you.
Campaign resources
Public-interest research compiled by students. Describing what a law or policy says is not a statement of the university's position, and not advice about your situation.
Bar eligibility and licensure
The single highest-stakes category on the site, and the one where a peer resource is least useful. The FlexJD FAQ states the programme is not F-1 visa eligible and that FlexJD students cannot currently sit for the bar exam in New York or Alabama because of limits those states place on distance education, and that some law schools may not accept transfer credits from the programme. Verify every licensure question with the jurisdiction's own admissions authority.

No professional advice

Nothing on this site is legal, academic, financial, disability-access, immigration, employment, or other professional advice, and reading it creates no advisory relationship. That matters most in the places where the material looks most like advice — a summary of a statute on a campaign page, a description of a loan term, a note about accommodations. Those are descriptions of public sources. Your situation is not a public source.

Who to ask, and for what

Phone numbers and channels below were read from Northeastern University School of Law's own pages on 29 August 2026. Route the question to the office that owns the answer — it is faster than asking a peer, and the answer is authoritative.

  • Degree requirements, registration, credits, leaves, reduced course loads: Academic and Student Affairs.
  • Aid, scholarships, loan consequences of withdrawing or dropping below half time: the Office of Financial Aid, 617.373.4620.
  • Bills, late fees, holds, payment plans: Student Accounts, 617.373.2270; the university's monthly payment plan is run by Nelnet Campus Commerce on 855.217.0551.
  • Admissions and application questions: Northeastern Law Admissions, 617.373.2395.
  • Anything you cannot place: the School of Law general line, 617.373.5149, 416 Huntington Avenue, Boston, Massachusetts 02115.
  • Bar admission, character and fitness, jurisdiction rules: the admissions authority of the jurisdiction itself, not the school and not this site.

Corrections

Corrections are the mechanism that makes an independent resource trustworthy rather than merely disclaimed. A useful correction names the page, quotes the statement that is wrong, and links the authoritative source that shows what is right. Material institutional corrections are logged in the changelog with what changed and why.

A person whose personal information appears on the site may ask for review, correction, or removal without providing a reason — see the privacy notice. Requests of that kind are handled ahead of everything else.

How records are labelled and what “verified” means here is set out on the verification page, and the governing rules are published in full in the source verification and freshness policy.
